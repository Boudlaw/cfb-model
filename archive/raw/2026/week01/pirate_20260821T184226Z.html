–

Our congratulations go out to the Michigan Wolverines and their fans for winning the 2026 College Basketball Division 1 National Championship, breaking the 26-year streak of no Big Ten teams winning the title. The Big Ten won both the men’s and women’s national championship as well as the last three college football national championships, which is an indicator that the largest athletic budgets mean a lot. Only one mid-major/low-major team, High Point, won a game in this year’s tournament, after none won last year. Money makes a huge difference, and this looks like a possible start to the Big Ten and SEC separating from the rest of college athletics and going out on their own.

The number of quality coaches that turned down the North Carolina job this year may be indicative of how the ACC is now being considered. Rather than the best basketball conference, as it was for many years, this league’s future is in serious doubt. It will be interesting to see what happens with Florida State and Clemson in any future conference realignment.

PiRate Ratings Bracketnomics Comparisons–Elite 8

Filed under: College Basketball — Tags: Arizona, Michigan, Illinois, Tennessee, Duke, Purdue, Connecticut, iowa, Elite 8, Comparisons — piratings @ 9:00 am

The 2026 Bracketnomics Edition has been somewhat of a disappointment compared to past seasons, but we still have five of our Super 11 remaining in the Elite 8, and we believe one of those five teams will cut down the nets in Indianapolis. Those Super 11 teams still alive are Arizona, Connecticut, Duke, Illinois, and Michigan. At least some of you have told us that you didn’t play our picks but used our data to make your own, and you probably have better brackets than we do.

Here are the Elite 8 Comparisons.

Illinois vs. Iowa

When these two teams played in Iowa City during the regular season, the Illini won a close game by having superior offensive efficiency. Illinois controlled the boards, while Iowa won the turnover margin. That’s exactly how this game should play out again. Illinois does not force turnovers, choosing to be more defensive inside and not gamble. Iowa applies more pressure on the perimeter, but they don’t have a massive inside presence. When Ben McCollum faces a team a second time in a season, his superior coaching usually is worth 5 to 8 extra points. But, Brad Underwood is also a superior coach, and that advantage has been neutralized. This is not a 50-50 game, but it is probably a 55-45 game in favor of the Illini with their superior offensive efficiency. Illinois won by 6 at Carver-Hawkeye Arena. They should repeat that feat to become the first team to qualify for the Final Four.

–

Arizona vs. Purdue

Before the tournament began, Charles Barkley called this year’s Big Dance, “The Arizona Invitational.” He’s starting to look like he was correct. Arizona has overwhelmed their opposition to get to this point, and this game looks like another double-digit win for the Wildcats. Arkansas played a rather decent game Thursday night, and they never had a chance against Arizona. The Wildcats couldn’t miss, either inside or outside (or at the foul line), and they scored an incredible 1.36 points per possession.

The great Al McGuire once said that a National Champion has to win a close game before the final game, or else they won’t be ready when they get that close game at the end. There is merit to that statement, but then if you are over the age of 65, you likely can remember the UCLA dynasty, where in some years, the Bruins easily breezed through the tournament with nothing but easy victories. Arizona is more like those UCLA teams the way they are playing right now, and until they spring a leak and lay an egg, there isn’t a team that can beat them. We don’t see that egg being laid tonight, and even though Purdue presents the toughest opponent the Wildcats have faced in the Big Dance, the Boilermakers will not play Spoilermakers in this one. Let’s go with ‘Zona by 10 to 15.

–

Michigan vs. Tennessee

Tennessee’s commanding rebounding advantage in this tournament has led the Big Orange to the Elite 8 for the third year in a row. And, for the third year in a row, the Vols are going home without making the Final Four. Michigan should limit the Tennessee’s inside strength, and the Vols don’t have a consistent enough backcourt to put up enough three-pointers to stay in this game. The Wolverines had a minor lapse after losing to Duke at Madison Square Garden in February, but the Maize and Blue are back to where they were before that game. The Wolverine defense will limit Tennessee to no more than 1.05 points per possession, and they should be around 1.15. That leads to about a 7-point victory to the Champions of the West.

–

Duke vs. Connecticut

Caleb Foster may not be able to play 35 minutes, but the 19 minutes he gave his team against St. John’s were the difference in the game. With Foster in the lineup getting the ball to Cameron Boozer and Isaiah Evans, Duke is at least 5 to 8 points better in those 19 minutes Foster plays.

Is Foster’s miraculous return the equivalent of Willis Reed’s 1970 miracle? No, it’s much better than that, because Reed had 4 points and 3 rebounds against Wilt Chamberlain, who had 21 points and 24 rebounds. The Knicks won that game because of Walt Frazier’s 19 assists and 36 points. Foster might go off for 15 points and 5 assists, while allowing Boozer and Evans to account for 40+ points and 15+ rebounds.

UConn is about on par with St. John’s, and with Foster having a game under his belt now, the Blue Devils should beat the Huskies by a tad more than they beat the Red Storm Friday night. With Foster playing 20 minutes per game, Duke’s Bracketnomics’ resume resembles a National Champion, but still it would be a tad behind Arizona and on par with Michigan.

PiRate Ratings NCAA Tournament Comparisons–Sweet 16

Filed under: College Basketball — Tags: Arizona, Arkansas, Connecticut, iowa, Michigan, Nebraska, Purdue, St. John's, Sweet 16, Texas — piratings @ 9:28 am

Even though we correctly picked 12 of the Sweet 16 in a year where many brackets have been spoiled, we consider this a down year for the PiRate Ratings Bracketnomics’ System. Our overall pick for the national champion has been eliminated, and the best we can hope for is to get three teams in the Final Four.

Our most glaring error this year has been in three-point percentage and turnovers. Apparently, the basketballs have been overinflated compared to the regular season. Longer shots that hit the rim are bouncing out when they normally might have stayed in the cylinder. The ball doesn’t dribble or pass the way it did during the regular season. Whether this will force us to modify the Bracketnomics in future seasons will be food for thought. Maybe, the multiple complaints will lead to this season being referred to as “inflategate.”

Here are the match-ups for the Sweet 16 games of Thursday and Friday.

Purdue vs. Texas

This is a fairly evenly matched game. Neither team has dominating numbers, and the winner of this game will not have a typical Final Four Bracketnomics Resume. Purdue has just a little better efficiency combined with better R+T Ratings numbers. But, Texas has the better interior defense, and if the three-point shots aren’t falling in this game, due somewhat to “inflategate,” the Longhorns would have the advantage in an inside-dominated game. The choice here is Purdue, because the Boilermaker experience in the Sweet 16 is worth something. Call it Purdue by 4 to 7 points with a score in the range of 74-69.

–

Nebraska vs. Iowa

These two teams split the regular season series, with the home team having the better overall stats in both games. One thing is for sure; possessions will be at a premium, as there likely will be no more than 65 possessions per team. Iowa has better rebounding, but Nebraska has better inside the paint defense. Where Iowa might have the best advantage is having Coach Ben McCollum on the bench in close games. McCollum has many games of tournament experience in the advanced rounds, and his Northwest Missouri State teams almost always won these types of games. It’s not a knock against Coach Fred Hoiberg. Let’s go with the Hawkeyes to win in a close game where neither team scores 70 points.

–

Arizona vs. Arkansas

If the trend of lower than expected outside shooting percentages hold up, this game will be a mismatch. Arizona has a commanding advantage inside in both field goal accuracy and offensive rebounding. Adding to this the news that Tommy Lloyd has indicated his desire to remain in Tucson and not be considered for the North Carolina job may fire up his team. This could easily be a double digit win for the Wildcats.

–

Houston vs. Illinois

Opposites attract, but in basketball opposites lead to an attractive game. Illinois has an incredible offensive efficiency ranking. Houston has an incredible defensive efficiency ranking. Illinois can bang it on the boards and dominate the rebounding numbers. Houston forces a lot of turnovers and can completely take an opponent out of their normal game plan. But, Houston is basically playing a home game, and that counts for about 3 points. Let’s go with the Cougars to take control in the second half and move onto the Elite 8 with a 6 to 12 point win.

–

Duke vs. St. John’s

St. John’s is winning without playing their best, while Duke has been fiddling at points during the first two rounds. When the two games were on the line, the Blue Devils put their opponents away. How much has the Caleb Wilson injury and having Patrick Ngongba playing at less than 100% affected Duke, and thus weaken their overall production? In the second halves of both tournament games, it didn’t look like it affected them much at all. It’s all about how far Cameron Boozer can take this team. In the Sweet 16, he can take Duke to the Elite 8.

–

Michigan vs. Alabama

Alabama is the exception to the rule in this tournament. The three’s rained down in their pasting of Texas Tech. Upon closer examination, more of their shots swished and did not hit the rim, so maybe it isn’t that much of an anomaly.

In Michigan’s loss to Wisconsin, the Badgers hit 15 three-pointers. It might take 15 for Alabama to win this game. Michigan has the defensive advantages inside the paint and on the boards. They even force more turnovers than the Tide does, and sometimes Alabama can get a little sloppy handling the ball. This one looks like a Wolverine win of more than 8 points and maybe the highest scoring game of the Sweet 16.

–

Connecticut vs. Michigan St.

Michigan State has improved since February, which is the sign of a great coach, and Tom Izzo is one of the best of all time. Danny Hurley is also a superior coach, so this game should be one where both teams bring their A-games, and in my opinion, this is the most interesting Sweet 16 game.

UConn has the better defensive efficiency, but the Spartans counter it with Final Four-worthy R+T Ratings. Both teams have Final Four talent and coaching staffs that know how to prepare their teams for the second weekend of the NCAA Tournament. This is a 50-50 game for sure, but Michigan State looks to me like a slightly better team. Sparty by 3.

–

Iowa St. vs. Tennessee

Tennessee is the best rebounding team in the nation, probably the best since North Carolina won the national championship in 2017. But, this Volunteer team is little other than a rebounding juggernaut.

Iowa State has superior offensive and defensive efficiency rankings. The Cyclones are the better three-point shooting team. They take care of the ball and force turnovers, while Tennessee has a negative TO Margin.

There are two more bonuses in ISU’s favor in this game. First, Coach TJ Otzeleberger made a firm comment that he is staying in Ames and is not a candidate for the North Carolina (or any other) coaching position. Players tend to get fired up over that. Next, Joshua Jefferson is likely to see action in this game, even if it is for a few minutes at a time and less than 20 total minutes. This also can fire up a team more than just having the talent back. Iowa State doesn’t need much to be a great team, and this puts them over the top. They might be outrebounded in this game, but not by enough to allow the Vols to score the extra points they will need per possession. Call it a Cyclone victory of 4 to 8 points.
