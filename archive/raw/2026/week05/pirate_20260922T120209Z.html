About The PiRate Ratings

For those new to the PiRate Ratings, the PiRate, Mean, and Bias ratings use the same data with three different algorithms to calculate a rating. Teams do not have set home field advantages; they differ for each game considering the circumstances (such as a team traveling 30 miles in one road game and then 2,500 miles in another road game). Ratings are updated by coming up with a true spread based on the statistical data and not just the final score. Team A might be a 17 point favorite and beat Team B 42-21, and Team C might have been a 17 point favorite and beat Team D 42-21, but the two games might be totally different. Team A might have led Team B 35-0 at the half and then played their reserves in the second half. Team C may have led Team D 28-21 as the fourth quarter started, and with Team D driving for the tying score, when a receiver bobbled a pass, and a defender caught it and ran for a touchdown to make it 35-21. Then trailing by 14, Team D might have failed to convert on 4th down inside their territory, and Team C had a short drive for another score. Team A would receive a lot more benefit than Team C, who most likely would lose some ratings points if the statistics showed a closer game.

The PiRate Ratings are Predictive (forward-looking). They are not rankings based on what a team has done so far, like the AP and Coaches Polls. They attempt to determine what will happen in a team’s next game.
